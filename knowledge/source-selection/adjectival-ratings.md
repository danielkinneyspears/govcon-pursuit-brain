# Adjectival Ratings

The qualitative ratings the government assigns to non-price factors during
source selection. The exact scale varies by agency and solicitation;
[[section-m]] states the scale that applies.

## Key facts

- A common technical / management scale:
  - **Outstanding** — meets requirements with an exceptional approach; multiple
    strengths, low risk.
  - **Good** — meets requirements with a thorough approach; some strengths,
    low to moderate risk.
  - **Acceptable** — meets requirements; adequate approach; risk no worse than
    moderate.
  - **Marginal** — does not clearly meet requirements; significant weaknesses.
  - **Unacceptable** — does not meet requirements; one or more deficiencies;
    unawardable.
- [[past-performance]] is typically rated on a separate **performance confidence**
  scale (e.g., Substantial / Satisfactory / Limited / No / Unknown confidence)
  rather than the technical scale.
- A rating is supported by the documented
  [[strengths-weaknesses-deficiencies]] for the factor.
- A [[red-team]] review assigns the ratings the proposal would draw from a
  real evaluator, against the actual Section M scale.
