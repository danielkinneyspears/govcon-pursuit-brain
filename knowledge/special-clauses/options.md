# Options

An **option** is a unilateral right of the government, expressed in the
contract, to **extend the contract's term** or **purchase additional
quantities** at the prices and on the terms stated. Options let the
government structure a multi-year acquisition without obligating future-year
funds today. Governed at FAR Subpart 17.2.

## Common clauses

- **52.217-5** — Evaluation of Options. Where stated, the government
  evaluates options at the time of award as part of price reasonableness.
- **52.217-7** — Option for Increased Quantity—Separately Priced Line Item.
- **52.217-8** — Option to Extend Services. The well-known **six-month
  bridge option** under which the government may extend services for up to
  six months at the prices in effect.
- **52.217-9** — Option to Extend the Term of the Contract. The standard
  multi-year option clause.

## Pricing implications

- Options are normally **separately priced CLINs**.
- Evaluators often score **Total Evaluated Price** = base + all options.
  Proposed prices for options are part of the competitive picture.
- **Escalation** assumptions on multi-year option prices are a common
  pricing lever in [[price-to-win]].

## Limitations

- Each option exercise must follow the procedures and notice periods in the
  clause; an option not exercised correctly is lost.
- Total contract performance with options exercised may not exceed limits
  in the clause (e.g., FAR 52.217-9's "5 years" limitation for options to
  extend, exclusive of bridge extensions).
