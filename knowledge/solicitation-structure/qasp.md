# Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan (QASP)

The government's plan for surveilling contractor performance — how it will
inspect, sample, measure, and accept work against the standards in a
[[performance-work-statement]]. The QASP is the operational counterpart to the
PWS: PWS says what good looks like; QASP says how the government will check.

## Key facts

- Surveillance methods include 100% inspection, random sampling, periodic
  surveillance, customer complaint mechanisms, and trend analysis.
- For each performance standard in the PWS, the QASP names the surveillance
  method, the acceptable quality level (AQL), and the consequence of falling
  below it (fee reduction, deduction, cure notice, etc.).
- The contractor's own quality control plan should anticipate and align with
  the QASP — what the government surveils, the contractor measures and
  controls first.
- Proposal sections on quality control should map to the QASP's measurement
  points so an evaluator sees them aligned.
