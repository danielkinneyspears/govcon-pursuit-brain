# Win Theme

A **repeated, substantiated message** that connects an offeror strength to a
customer concern and to a scored [[section-m]] factor. Win themes are what
separate a compliant proposal from a winning one.

## The five parts

A win theme is a structured claim, not a slogan:

1. **Hot button** — the customer concern or priority the theme serves.
2. **Discriminator** — the validated [[discriminator]] it rests on.
3. **Benefit** — what the customer *gets*, in the customer's terms.
4. **Proof** — past performance, metrics, named facts, certifications.
5. **Factor** — the Section M evaluation factor the theme is scored under.

Stated as a sentence: *Because [discriminator with proof], the customer gets
[benefit], which addresses [hot button]* — built to be scored under [factor].

## Threading

A theme is *threaded*: it appears in the executive summary, recurs as the
governing message of the relevant proposal section, and is reinforced by
proof throughout. One theme, stated consistently, repeated where it counts.

Most proposals carry **three to five** win themes — enough to cover the major
evaluation factors, few enough that each is actually remembered.

## Common failure modes

- Themes built on **table stakes** (strengths every competitor also has) —
  see the rarity test in [[discriminator]].
- **Feature lists** that never reach the customer benefit.
- **Unprovable themes** with no evidence.
- Theme **inflation** — fifteen "themes," none repeated.
- **Orphan themes** that map to no Section M factor.
