# Bid/No-Bid Decision

The **gated decision** to pursue (or decline) a federal opportunity, based on
the probability of win and the value. The expensive mistake in federal BD is
not losing a bid — it is spending bid-and-proposal money on bids that were
never winnable. The bid/no-bid decision exists to prevent that.

## The decision components

- **Knockout checks** — set-aside ineligibility, missing mandatory
  qualifications (clearance, certification, NAICS size standard, vehicle),
  unresolvable OCI, no real funding, no time to comply, categorically
  unacceptable terms, strategic veto. Any triggered knockout drives a no-bid.
- **Weighted scorecard** — typically across customer relationship, opportunity
  fit / strategic alignment, competitive position, solution and delivery
  capability, financial and business attractiveness, and risk / executability.
- **Probability of win (pWin)** — an honest, evidence-based percentage,
  starting from a base rate (1/N for N credible bidders) and adjusted up or
  down for documented relationship, shaping, real discriminators, and a
  reachable price-to-win.

## Outcomes

- **Bid** — pursue and propose.
- **No-bid** — decline; record why so the lesson is reusable.
- **Conditional bid** — pursue only if specific, named conditions are met by
  a specific date.

## Gate cadence

The decision is made repeatedly at successive gates as uncertainty falls —
typically **pursuit decision** (pre-RFP), **preliminary bid decision** (at
draft RFP or imminent RFP), and **bid validation** (post-RFP).
