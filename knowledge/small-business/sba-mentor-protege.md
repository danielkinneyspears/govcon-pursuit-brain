# SBA Mentor-Protégé Program

The SBA Mentor-Protégé Program allows a **small business protégé** to partner
with a **more experienced mentor** (which may itself be a large business) to
gain capacity and win federal contracts.

## Key facts

- A mentor and protégé can form a **joint venture** that qualifies as small
  for any small business contract for which the protégé qualifies — including
  set-asides under [[8a-program|8(a)]], [[sdvosb]], [[wosb-edwosb|WOSB/EDWOSB]],
  and [[hubzone]].
- **Protégé performs at least 40%** of the work performed by the joint
  venture (13 CFR § 125.8).
- The joint venture must submit annual evaluation reports, annual
  performance-of-work statements, and project-end performance-of-work
  statements to SBA and the contracting agencies.
- A mentor (and its parent/subsidiary group) generally may not have more than
  **three protégés** at one time.
- A **final rule effective January 16, 2025** clarified that when an agency
  requires past performance and/or experience of a protégé joint-venture
  partner, the requirement must be at a **reduced level** compared to other
  offerors — preserving the program's purpose.

## Why it matters

- A mentor's past performance, scale, and resources help the protégé compete
  for work it could not credibly pursue alone.
- A mentor gains a path into set-aside work through the joint venture without
  needing the underlying socioeconomic status itself.
