# Nonmanufacturer Rule

For **small-business set-aside supply contracts**, the **Nonmanufacturer Rule
(NMR)** requires that the small business be the manufacturer of the supplied
end item, **unless** the firm meets specific nonmanufacturer requirements.

## Nonmanufacturer requirements

To furnish a product it does not manufacture, the small business must:

1. **Not exceed 500 employees** (or the size standard applicable to the
   underlying manufacturing NAICS, whichever is lower) — and otherwise
   qualify as small.
2. Be **primarily engaged** in the retail or wholesale trade and normally
   sell the type of item being supplied.
3. Take **ownership or possession** of the item with its own personnel,
   equipment, or facilities consistent with industry practice.
4. Supply the product of a **small business manufacturer** made in the United
   States, **unless a waiver applies**.

## Waivers

SBA can issue **individual** waivers (for a specific procurement) or **class**
waivers (for a class of items, when no small-business manufacturer is
available). Class waivers are published by SBA and revisited periodically.

> [!note]
> For acquisitions valued at or below the SAT, the small-business
> manufacturer requirement and the nonmanufacturer rule are relaxed under
> certain conditions; check the current rule and the solicitation language.
