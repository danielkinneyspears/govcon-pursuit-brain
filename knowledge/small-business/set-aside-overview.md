# Small Business Set-Asides — Overview

The federal government limits competition for many acquisitions to **small
business concerns** to support small-business participation. Set-asides are
either by size alone or by a socioeconomic category.

## Categories

- **Small business set-aside** — open to any business that qualifies as small
  under the applicable NAICS [[size-standards-and-naics|size standard]].
- **8(a) Business Development** ([[8a-program]]) — for small businesses owned
  by socially and economically disadvantaged citizens; SBA-administered.
- **HUBZone** ([[hubzone]]) — for small businesses located in and employing
  staff from Historically Underutilized Business Zones.
- **Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB)** ([[sdvosb]]) —
  small businesses at least 51% owned by service-disabled veterans recognized
  by the VA.
- **Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB)** and **Economically Disadvantaged
  Women-Owned Small Business (EDWOSB)** ([[wosb-edwosb]]) — small businesses
  owned by women, with EDWOSB adding an economic-disadvantage test.

## Triggering rules

- **Automatic small-business reservation** (FAR 19.502-2(a)) — acquisitions
  above the [[micro-purchase-threshold|MPT]] and at or below the
  [[simplified-acquisition-threshold|SAT]] are reserved for small business.
- **Rule of Two** (FAR 19.502-2(b)) — above the SAT, set aside for small
  business when there is a reasonable expectation of receiving offers from
  two or more responsible small business concerns at fair market prices.
- Within a small-business set-aside, the contracting officer may further
  set aside to a specific socioeconomic category if a Rule of Two is met
  within that category.

> [!warning] Certifications required
> Set-asides in the 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB/EDWOSB, and HUBZone programs require
> active **SBA-issued certification** at the time of offer and award.
> Self-certification is no longer accepted for these programs.
