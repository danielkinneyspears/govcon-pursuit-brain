# Limitation on Subcontracting

On set-aside contracts, the **Limitation on Subcontracting** clause caps how
much of the award value may go to subcontractors that are not similarly
situated. The clause implements 13 CFR § 125.6.

## Key thresholds

The clause caps subcontracting to **non-similarly-situated entities** at
**50% of the amount paid by the Government for contract performance**:

- **Services (except construction):** ≤ 50% to non-similarly-situated
  subcontractors.
- **Supplies (other than nonmanufacturer procurements):** ≤ 50% (excluding
  cost of materials) to non-similarly-situated subcontractors.
- **General construction:** ≤ 85% to non-similarly-situated subcontractors
  (excluding cost of materials).
- **Special trade construction:** ≤ 75% to non-similarly-situated
  subcontractors (excluding cost of materials).

## Similarly situated entities

A **similarly situated entity** is a first-tier subcontractor that has the
**same small business program status** that qualified the prime for the award
(small, 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB/EDWOSB, HUBZone) and is small under the NAICS code
the prime assigned to the subcontract.

Work performed by similarly situated entities **does not count** against the
prime's 50% cap. Any further subcontracting by a similarly situated entity to
non-similarly-situated subcontractors **does** count.

> [!warning] Compliance is per-contract and audited
> Compliance is measured at contract completion (or annually on long-term
> contracts). Failure to comply can result in negative past performance,
> liquidated damages, suspension, and debarment.
