# Size Standards and NAICS

To qualify as a **small business** for federal contracting, a firm must meet
the size standard applicable to the **North American Industry Classification
System (NAICS) code** assigned to the procurement. SBA publishes the size
standards table; codes and standards are updated periodically.

## How size is measured

- **Receipts-based** standards (services): average annual receipts over the
  prior 5 completed fiscal years (5-year averaging adopted in the Runway
  Extension Act).
- **Employee-based** standards (manufacturing, some others): average number of
  employees over the trailing 24-month period.
- Some industries have **alternative** size standards (e.g., specific dollar
  caps for certain industries or hybrid measures).

## Affiliation

- The size of a firm includes the size of its **affiliates** (13 CFR § 121.103).
  Common bases for affiliation: ownership, common management, identity of
  interest, and the "totality of the circumstances" test.
- A joint venture is treated as small if all members are small, with
  exceptions for [[sba-mentor-protege]] joint ventures where the mentor may be
  large.

## Recertification

- Self-certification of size occurs at the time of offer on the procurement,
  with recertification triggered by certain events (long-term contracts,
  mergers, etc.; see 13 CFR § 121.404).

## When SBA-issued certification is required

- For [[8a-program|8(a)]], [[sdvosb]], [[wosb-edwosb|WOSB/EDWOSB]], and
  [[hubzone]], the firm must hold the **SBA-issued certification** at the time
  of offer and award (self-certification not accepted).

> [!warning] 2026 adjustments
> SBA periodically adjusts size standards. Firms borderline in 2025 may find
> themselves over or under in 2026; recheck the SBA Size Standards table
> against the NAICS code on the solicitation before bidding.
