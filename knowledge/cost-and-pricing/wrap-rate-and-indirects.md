# Wrap Rate and Indirect Rates

A **wrap rate** is the fully burdened hourly rate — direct labor plus
applicable indirect costs (fringe, overhead, general and administrative) plus
fee. The wrap rate is what the government effectively pays per labor hour.

## The indirect rate stack

A typical labor rate build (varies by company cost-accounting structure):

```
Direct Labor (hourly base)
+ Fringe (paid leave, benefits, taxes — applied to direct labor)
+ Overhead (project / facility costs — applied to direct labor + fringe)
+ G&A (general and administrative — applied to direct labor + fringe + overhead)
+ Fee (profit)
= Wrap Rate (fully burdened hourly rate)
```

The exact applicable bases depend on the company's cost accounting policies
and any approved indirect-rate structure.

## Forward Pricing Rate Agreements (FPRAs)

For major defense and other contractors, DCMA negotiates **Forward Pricing
Rate Agreements** that set indirect rate ranges for proposal use, reducing
back-and-forth on each bid (FAR 15.407-3).

## In proposals and evaluation

- Wrap rates appear in [[time-and-materials]] contracts directly (as the
  fixed hourly rates).
- For [[cost-reimbursement-contracts]] and [[firm-fixed-price]], rates are
  built into the [[basis-of-estimate]] and the priced CLINs.
- Wide variation in wrap rates between offerors is a common driver of
  price spread; lower-overhead firms compete on price; large primes compete
  on past performance, scale, and discriminators despite higher overhead.
