# Cost Realism Analysis

The independent review and evaluation of specific elements of an offeror's
proposed cost estimate to determine whether the estimated proposed cost
elements are realistic for the work to be performed, reflect a clear
understanding of the requirements, and are consistent with the unique methods
of performance and materials described in the offeror's technical proposal
(FAR 15.404-1(d)).

## When it is required

- **Required** on **cost-reimbursement** contracts. The probable cost shall be
  determined and used for evaluation (FAR 15.404-1(d)(2)).
- **Permitted** on **fixed-price incentive** contracts or, in exceptional
  circumstances, on other competitive **fixed-price** contracts, when the
  solicitation expressly provides for it (FAR 15.404-1(d)(3)).

## How it affects evaluation

- If the proposed cost is judged **unrealistic**, the contracting officer
  adjusts it **upward** for evaluation purposes to reflect the "most probable
  cost" — so an offeror cannot win a cost-reimbursement competition by simply
  bidding implausibly low.
- An unrealistic cost can also signal **performance risk** and lower the
  offeror's [[past-performance|performance-confidence]] rating.

> [!warning] The lever is the solution, not the numbers
> To be evaluated at a lower cost, propose an approach that **genuinely costs
> less** — fewer hours, a leaner labor mix the work supports — not the same
> approach with shaved numbers. Cost realism cuts through cost-only changes.
