# GAO Bid Protest

A **bid protest** filed with the U.S. **Government Accountability Office
(GAO)** — the most common forum for protesting a federal procurement action.
Procedures are at FAR 33.104 and 4 CFR Part 21.

## Timeliness

- **Protests based on alleged improprieties in a solicitation** that are
  apparent prior to bid opening or the time set for receipt of initial
  proposals must be filed **before** that time (4 CFR § 21.2(a)(1)).
- **In all other cases**, a protest must be filed not later than **10 days**
  after the basis of protest is known or should have been known, whichever is
  earlier.
- For procurements where a required postaward debrief was timely requested,
  the protest must be filed **not later than 10 days after the date on which
  the debriefing is held**.

## The CICA stay

A timely post-award protest filed within either **10 days of award** or **5
days of the debriefing date offered to the protester**, whichever is later,
triggers a **CICA stay** — performance of the contract must be suspended
until the protest is resolved (FAR 33.104(b), 31 U.S.C. § 3553(d)).

## What GAO does

- **Reviews** the protest record and the agency report.
- **Recommends** corrective action; GAO's decision is technically a
  recommendation, but agencies almost always follow it.
- **Typical timeline:** 100 calendar days for a written decision; 65 days for
  an express option in limited cases.

## Forums and choice

- GAO is the **lower-cost, faster** forum and the most common choice.
- The [[court-of-federal-claims-protest|Court of Federal Claims]] has broader
  jurisdiction and issues binding orders; some protesters file there instead
  (or after a GAO loss).
- [[agency-level-protest|Agency-level protests]] are also available and are
  the only path for certain low-dollar matters.
