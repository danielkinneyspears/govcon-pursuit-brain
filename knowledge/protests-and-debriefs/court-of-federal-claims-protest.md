# Court of Federal Claims (COFC) Bid Protest

A **bid protest** filed at the U.S. **Court of Federal Claims (COFC)** under
the **Tucker Act**, 28 U.S.C. § 1491(b). COFC has broader jurisdiction than
[[gao-bid-protest|GAO]] and issues **binding orders** rather than
recommendations.

## Jurisdiction

The Tucker Act grants the COFC jurisdiction over:

1. **Procurement solicitations** — challenges to the solicitation itself.
2. **Awards for procurement** — challenges to the award decision.
3. Any alleged **violation of statute or regulation in connection with a
   procurement or proposed procurement** — the broadest jurisdictional grant.

The third prong gives COFC reach beyond what CICA allows the GAO to consider.

## Differences from GAO

| Factor | GAO | COFC |
|---|---|---|
| Decision type | Recommendation | Binding court order |
| Cost / time | Lower / faster | Higher / slower |
| Discovery | Limited | More available |
| Forum-shopping consideration | First-line forum | Often used after GAO loss or where broader claims exist |
| Task-order protests | Limited (FASA bar exceptions) | Generally barred for orders (with narrow scope/period exception) |

## Practical considerations

- A protester can file at GAO first and then COFC if unsuccessful, subject to
  timing rules.
- COFC injunctive relief requires meeting the traditional preliminary
  injunction factors.
- Filing at COFC does **not** automatically trigger the CICA stay; a separate
  injunction motion is needed.
