# NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)

The **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)** is a voluntary
framework for managing risks across the AI lifecycle. NIST also publishes a
**Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1)** with practices tailored to
generative-AI risks.

## Four core functions

The AI RMF organizes practice into four functions:

- **Govern** — establish a culture of risk management for AI: roles,
  policies, processes.
- **Map** — establish the context, intended use, and risks of the AI system.
- **Measure** — analyze, assess, and monitor AI risks and performance.
- **Manage** — prioritize and act on identified risks.

## Generative AI Profile

The Generative AI Profile applies the four functions to generative-AI
specifics, including risks like confabulation, content authenticity, data
governance, harmful bias, IP exposure, and human-AI interaction risks.

## Status in federal policy (2026)

- Under the Biden administration (M-24-10 / M-24-18), agencies were
  **encouraged or directed** to use the AI RMF and the Generative AI Profile.
- Under the current OMB memos ([[omb-m-25-21]] and [[omb-m-25-22]]), explicit
  references to the AI RMF were **removed**. Agencies are still free to use
  the framework; it is just not centrally mandated.
- Solicitations may still cite the AI RMF — check Section M of any AI-related
  solicitation before assuming.

## Why it still matters

- A widely understood **vocabulary** for AI risk that maps to enterprise
  governance.
- Aligns with many state and international AI-risk regimes.
- Useful as a **defensible framework** for an offeror's AI governance
  story, even if not centrally mandated.
