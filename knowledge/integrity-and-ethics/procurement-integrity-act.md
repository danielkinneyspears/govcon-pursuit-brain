# Procurement Integrity Act (FAR 3.104)

The **Procurement Integrity Act (PIA)** is the federal statute that protects
the integrity of source selection. It restricts the **disclosure** and **use**
of **contractor bid or proposal information** and **source-selection
information** before contract award, and imposes related restrictions on
employment discussions, gratuities, and post-employment activities.
Implemented in FAR Subpart 3.104.

## The core prohibition

A present or former federal official, and any person acting for or on behalf
of the United States with respect to a procurement, must not **knowingly
disclose** contractor bid or proposal information or source-selection
information before award (FAR 3.104-3). Offerors and others must not
**knowingly obtain** such information.

## What counts as source-selection information

- The [[igce|independent government cost estimate]].
- Source-selection plans and evaluation criteria not yet public.
- Technical evaluation reports, cost evaluation reports, and rankings.
- Competitive-range determinations.
- Competing offerors' proposals and pricing.
- Source-selection decision documents prior to award.

## What this means for an offeror

- **Never seek, accept, or use** non-public information about the evaluation,
  the IGCE, the competitive range, or other offerors' proposals before award.
- If a federal contact offers such information, the correct action is to
  **decline and report it** — not to use it.
- Treat any non-public capture-phase intelligence with suspicion as to
  provenance; if it might be source-selection-sensitive, **quarantine** it.

## Marking and handling

- Documents containing source-selection information are marked
  "**Source Selection Information — See FAR 2.101 and 3.104**" (FAR
  3.104-4).
- Contractor bid or proposal information submitted to the government is
  protected from disclosure.

> [!warning] Disqualification and criminal exposure
> Violations can result in proposal disqualification, criminal penalties, and
> civil sanctions. The exposure runs in both directions — the federal
> official disclosing and the offeror receiving.
