# Organizational Conflict of Interest (OCI)

An **Organizational Conflict of Interest** exists when, because of other
activities, relationships, or contracts, a contractor is or may be unable to
render impartial assistance or advice to the government; the contractor's
objectivity in performing the work is or might be otherwise impaired; or the
contractor has an unfair competitive advantage (FAR 2.101 definition).
Implemented at FAR Subpart 9.5.

## The three types

- **Unequal access to information** — the contractor has had access to
  non-public information not available to other competitors. Example: a
  contractor that supports the program office and saw the unreleased
  acquisition strategy now bids on the work.
- **Biased ground rules** — the contractor helped set the ground rules for
  the procurement (wrote the SOW, drafted the evaluation criteria) and then
  bids on it.
- **Impaired objectivity** — the contractor's work on a contract puts it in
  the position of evaluating itself or a closely related entity.

## Mitigation and waiver

The contracting officer is required to **identify and evaluate** potential
OCIs and to **avoid, neutralize, or mitigate** them. Common mitigations
include firewalls, divestiture, recusal, and limitations on future bidding.
A **waiver** in writing by the head of the contracting activity is available
when mitigation is not feasible (FAR 9.503).

## Recent and pending changes

- On **January 15, 2025**, the FAR Council proposed a wholesale revision of
  OCI rules to implement the Preventing Organizational Conflicts of Interest
  in Federal Acquisition Act, including moving OCI from FAR Part 9 to FAR
  Part 3, new definitions, and new clauses. Implementation is ongoing —
  verify the current status of the rulemaking before relying on the location.

> [!warning] OCI is for counsel
> An unresolved OCI can disqualify an offer. Identification is for the team;
> assessment of OCI merit and mitigation strategy is for contracts and legal
> counsel.
