# Task Order Protest Rights

Protest rights for orders placed under multiple-award IDIQs are deliberately
narrowed (sometimes called the **FASA bar**). Most task-order awards cannot
be protested at all.

## Key facts

- **General rule:** a protest of an order is permitted only if (1) the order
  is alleged to be beyond the scope, maximum value, or period of the underlying
  contract; or (2) the order value exceeds the statutory threshold (see
  below).
- **Threshold for DoD, NASA, and Coast Guard orders:** protests of orders
  valued at more than **$25 million** are permitted at the [[gao-bid-protest|GAO]]
  only (10 U.S.C. § 3406(f)).
- **Threshold for civilian agency orders:** protests of orders valued at more
  than **$10 million** are permitted at the GAO only (41 U.S.C. § 4106(f)).
- These protests must go to **GAO only**, not to the
  [[court-of-federal-claims-protest|Court of Federal Claims]] (with the narrow
  scope/period exception).
- A protest of the underlying multiple-award IDIQ contract itself (an
  on-ramp, a make-or-break solicitation) follows the normal rules — the FASA
  bar applies only to orders.

> [!warning] Thresholds change
> The statutory thresholds for task-order protests are amended periodically.
> Verify the current amounts before filing.
