# Task Order Protest Rights

Protest rights for orders placed under multiple-award IDIQs are deliberately
narrowed (sometimes called the **FASA bar**). Most task-order awards can't
be protested at all.

## Key facts

- **General rule:** a protest of an order is permitted only if (1) the order
  is alleged to increase the scope, period, or maximum value of the
  underlying contract; or (2) the order value exceeds the statutory
  threshold below.
- **DoD, NASA, and Coast Guard orders:** protests of orders valued above
  **$35 million** are permitted at the [[gao-bid-protest|GAO]] only (10
  U.S.C. § 3406(f)). Section 885 of the FY2025 NDAA raised this from $25
  million.
- **Civilian agency orders:** protests of orders valued above **$10
  million** are permitted at the GAO only (41 U.S.C. § 4106(f)). Unchanged.
- **Scope, period, or value claims** can be brought at GAO at any order
  value.
- These protests go to **GAO only**, not to the
  [[court-of-federal-claims-protest|Court of Federal Claims]], except for the
  scope/period/value ground.
- Below the thresholds, the agency's **task order ombudsman** reviews
  complaints. It isn't a protest forum.
- The order protest rules now sit at **FAR 16.508** under the
  [[revolutionary-far-overhaul]] deviation (formerly 16.505(a)(10)).
- A protest of the underlying multiple-award contract itself (an on-ramp
  or the original competition) follows the normal rules. The FASA bar
  applies only to orders.
- Section 885 also directed GAO and DoD to study changes such as enhanced
  pleading standards and a "loser pays" rule for DoD protests. Watch for
  follow-on legislation.

> [!warning] Thresholds change
> The statutory thresholds are amended periodically. Verify the current
> amounts before filing.
