# Fair Opportunity (FAR 16.507, formerly 16.505)

The procedure for placing task or delivery orders under multiple-award
indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity ([[idiq]]) contracts and BPAs: each
awardee must get a **fair opportunity** to be considered for each order over
the [[micro-purchase-threshold]].

> [!warning] Renumbered by the FAR overhaul
> Under the [[revolutionary-far-overhaul]] Part 16 deviation, the old 16.505
> was split. Post-award ordering procedures and order contents are now
> **16.506**. Fair opportunity is **16.507**. Order protests are **16.508**.
> Contracts and solicitations written before the deviation still cite
> 16.505. Use the numbering the document in front of you uses.

## Key facts

- Fair opportunity is required for each order above the MPT unless an
  exception applies. Exceptions include urgency, only one awardee capable
  of the unique or specialized work, a logical follow-on to a competed
  order, satisfying a minimum guarantee, a small business set-aside among
  the small business awardees, and a statute that authorizes or requires
  the order.
- The rules are tiered by order value:
  - **Above the MPT (16.507-3):** fair opportunity, with broad discretion
    on how it's run.
  - **Above the [[simplified-acquisition-threshold]] (16.507-4):** fair
    notice of the intent to place the order to all awardees, and the basis
    for selection.
  - **Above $7.5 million (16.507-5):** a clear statement of requirements, the
    significant evaluation factors and subfactors with their relative
    importance, a reasonable response period, documented evaluation, and a
    post-award debrief on request. The threshold was $6 million before
    2025-10-01.
- Streamlined procedures: no full FAR Part 15 process. Requests are often a
  Request for Task Order Proposal or an RFQ with compressed instructions.

> [!note]
> Protest rights on task orders are restricted. See
> [[task-order-protest-rights]].
