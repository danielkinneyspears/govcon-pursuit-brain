# ITAR and EAR (Export Controls)

Two U.S. export-control regimes that govern the export, re-export, and certain
domestic transfers of defense and dual-use items, technical data, software,
and services. **ITAR and EAR are mutually exclusive**: an item subject to one
is not subject to the other.

## ITAR — International Traffic in Arms Regulations

- **Agency:** Department of State, **Directorate of Defense Trade Controls
  (DDTC)**.
- **Regulation:** 22 CFR Parts 120–130.
- **List:** **United States Munitions List (USML)** — defense articles,
  defense services, and related technical data **specifically designed,
  developed, configured, or adapted for military use**.
- **Posture:** Most exports of USML items require a license; some exemptions
  exist.
- **Registration:** Manufacturers, exporters, and brokers of USML items must
  **register with DDTC** even if no license is presently needed.

## EAR — Export Administration Regulations

- **Agency:** Department of Commerce, **Bureau of Industry and Security
  (BIS)**.
- **Regulation:** 15 CFR Parts 730–774.
- **List:** **Commerce Control List (CCL)** — "dual-use" and certain
  commercial items, classified by **Export Control Classification Number
  (ECCN)**.
- **CCL structure:** Ten categories (0–9, e.g., Nuclear Materials, Materials,
  Materials Processing, Electronics, Computers, Telecommunications, Sensors,
  Navigation, Marine, Propulsion) × five product groups (A Equipment,
  B Test/Production, C Materials, D Software, E Technology).
- **Posture:** Licensing varies by ECCN, destination country, end-user, and
  end-use; many items are EAR99 (a residual category, generally export-allowed
  with restrictions on embargoed destinations and prohibited end uses).

## For federal contractors

- ITAR/EAR compliance is a contractor responsibility — DDTC and BIS regulate;
  agencies require contractor compliance.
- **Deemed exports** — release of controlled technical data to a foreign
  person within the United States can be an export and may require a license.
- Cross-references CUI: certain export-controlled material is marked
  `CUI//EXPT` and combines CUI safeguarding with export-control restrictions
  (see [[cui]]).
