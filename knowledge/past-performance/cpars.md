# Contractor Performance Assessment Reporting System (CPARS)

CPARS is the **government's system of record** for contractor performance
information. Agencies enter Contractor Performance Assessment Reports (CPARs)
in CPARS, and source-selection officials read CPARS during
[[past-performance]] evaluations.

## Key facts

- **PPIRS retired** in January 2019; all past-performance data merged into
  **CPARS.gov**. Source-selection officials access CPARS data directly.
- A CPAR rates the contractor's performance across factors such as
  **quality**, **schedule**, **cost control**, **management**, **utilization
  of small business**, and **regulatory compliance**. Each factor uses an
  adjectival scale (Exceptional, Very Good, Satisfactory, Marginal,
  Unsatisfactory).
- The contractor may **review and respond** to a CPAR before it is finalized;
  contracting officers consider the response.
- Data is retained for **3 years after contract completion** (6 years for
  Architect-Engineer and Construction contracts).

## Recent / pending changes (2026)

- The **2026 NDAA includes CPARS reforms** that the government is rolling out
  starting in late 2026, including a shift toward a "negative event" model in
  certain contexts (focus on material performance failures rather than the
  traditional narrative ratings). Watch for implementing rulemaking.

> [!warning] CPARS is read directly by evaluators
> Evaluators routinely pull CPARS independently. A past-performance volume
> that omits a known CPARS problem and lets the evaluator find it
> independently is far more damaging than one that addresses the problem and
> its corrective action.
